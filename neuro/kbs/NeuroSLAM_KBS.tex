\begin{abstract}
    \sloppy
    Monocular visual-inertial odometry recovers the shape of a trajectory but not its metric scale: the estimate is defined only up to a similarity transform, so the scale error enters every downstream quantity, and drift can be removed only where the vehicle recognises ground it has already mapped. This paper presents NeuroLocMap, a brain-inspired localization-mapping framework formulated in a four-degree-of-freedom (4-DoF) state, which couples vestibular--visual complementary fusion with hippocampal--entorhinal spatial representations. The pipeline combines dual-stream visual processing for appearance and motion, inertial integration, a three-dimensional grid-cell attractor for position encoding and an experience map for loop closure, all built on a fixed-dimension 10-state EKF core. Two train-once components are attached downstream of the filter: a scale-only monocular correction implemented as a lightweight head over a frozen depth model, and an embedding-based loop-closure channel. Across five scenarios, four CARLA towns (Town05/03/07/10HD) and one real highway sequence (KITTI~07), the full system reduces error against the 10-state EKF visual-inertial baseline on every scenario under both evaluation calibers: 16.5--59.3\% after a full-Sim(3) refit, which absorbs global scale and therefore bounds the achievable gain from above, and 1.5--33.9\% under the rigid SE(3) caliber, where an incorrect metric scale cannot be absorbed. On a closed-loop benchmark the experience-map re-anchoring reduces terminal displacement from 842.54~m to 55.92~m. The reference implementation runs at 39--76~FPS on a CPU without GPU acceleration, and its learned components transfer from simulation without per-domain retraining; the sim-to-real gap of the scale head is measured rather than assumed away.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Localization and mapping in three-dimensional environments underpins autonomous driving, mobile robotics and intelligent transportation systems~\cite{cadena2016past}. Practical systems combine cameras, inertial measurement units and, where available, satellite positioning to obtain a pose estimate, and the estimate itself is almost always produced by an optimiser or a filter~\cite{saputra2018visual}. Two costs follow from that choice. The first is transparency: the map is an artefact of the solver, and the quantities that produced it, feature tracks and marginal covariances, carry no spatial meaning that a human or a planner can read. The second is computation: where visual features enter the state, the per-update cost grows with their number, which is the customary obstacle to deployment on embedded hardware.

Biological navigation pays neither cost. Bats localise in three dimensions by combining visual and vestibular signals with spatially selective cells of the hippocampal formation~\cite{jeffery2013navigating,hafting2005microstructure,taube1990head,finkelstein2016three,solstad2008representation}. The circuits involved, grid cells, head-direction cells and place cells, hold position and orientation in a representation that is at once metric, low-dimensional and readable as a map. The appeal of this architecture for robotics is therefore not metaphorical: it suggests a state whose dimension is fixed by the encoding rather than by scene complexity.

Transferring the principle to a working system meets three difficulties. Most existing bio-inspired frameworks are planar, whereas road and indoor deployment requires a three-dimensional representation together with heading. Their visual front-ends rest on intensity templates whose discrimination degrades under illumination and viewpoint change, so place recognition fails where it is most needed. And fusion, where present, is either ad hoc or borrowed from tightly coupled optimisation, which reintroduces the feature-dependent cost that the biological architecture avoids.

This paper presents NeuroLocMap, a brain-inspired localization-mapping framework formulated in the 4-DoF pose $(x,y,z,\psi)$: a dual-stream visual front-end separates appearance from motion (ventral for scene recognition, dorsal for motion extraction), a vestibular front-end processes inertial data, and the two are combined by a frequency-domain fusion core that instantiates complementary filtering as a fixed-dimension (10-state) EKF. Position is encoded by three-dimensional grid cells, heading by multilayered head-direction cells, and place by a multilayer experience map that serves as an episodic memory for recognition and drift correction. The restriction to four degrees of freedom is deliberate. On road and indoor surfaces the orientation is dominated by yaw, while pitch and roll stay within the range imposed by vehicle dynamics (below $5^\circ$), so a 4-DoF state captures the motion that matters at a fraction of the state dimension of a full pose graph~\cite{milford2004ratslam,milford2010persistent}.

The contributions of this paper are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Architecture.} Dual-stream visual processing, vestibular modelling and hippocampal--entorhinal spatial representations are coupled into a single 4-DoF pipeline, with the filter, the spatial cells and the experience map operating on one state rather than as stacked modules.
    \item \emph{Visual front-end.} An explicit template-creation operator replaces the fixed template set of RatSLAM; across the evaluated sequences the template count rises from 5 to 964, and it is this increase that sustains place discrimination under illumination and viewpoint change.
    \item \emph{Fusion core.} A fixed-dimension (10-state), loosely coupled EKF whose gated branches are applied in a fixed order (Section~\ref{sec:deployed_filter}), so that the per-update cost is independent of the number of tracked features. Two train-once corrections, a scale-only head over a frozen depth model and a learned-embedding loop-closure line, sit downstream of the filter and do not write back into it.
    \item \emph{Evaluation.} On five scenarios (CARLA Town05/03/07/10HD and KITTI~07) the full system reduces error against the 10-state EKF baseline on all five under the rigid SE(3) caliber, where an unrecovered scale cannot be absorbed ($+$1.5--33.9\%), and on the closed-loop benchmark it reduces terminal displacement from 842.54~m to 55.92~m. The map produced is a topological experience graph, inspectable by construction rather than by post-hoc visualisation; this is treated as a property of the design, not as a separate claim.
\end{itemize}

In knowledge-based terms, the experience map produced by our pipeline serves as an explicit and interpretable topological knowledge representation, and loop closure operates as retrieval over this representation together with consistency-based correction of it.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

All simulated sequences come from CARLA towns collected by ourselves; no third-party SLAM benchmark (e.g., EuRoC) is evaluated, so cross-dataset generalisation of the map and gate parameters remains to be shown.

This paper presented NeuroLocMap, a brain-inspired localization-mapping framework formulated in a 4-DoF state and built on a fixed-dimension 10-state EKF core, in which dual-stream visual processing, vestibular--visual complementary fusion, a three-dimensional grid-cell attractor and multilayered head-direction cells operate as one pipeline rather than as stacked modules. Across four CARLA towns and one real highway sequence the full system reduced error against the 10-state EKF visual-inertial baseline on all five scenarios under both evaluation calibers, and on the closed-loop benchmark the experience-map re-anchoring reduced terminal displacement from 842.54~m to 55.92~m. The reference implementation ran at 39--76~FPS on a CPU without GPU acceleration, with learned components trained once in simulation and deployed without per-domain retraining.

Three limitations bound these claims. The 4-DoF state assumes that pitch and roll remain small, which holds for road vehicles and indoor floors but not for aerial or legged platforms. The scale head is trained on simulated imagery, and its sim-to-real gap, although measured, remains the weakest link in the metric-scale chain. The node-level relaxation of Section~\ref{sec:loop_closure} is a reference formulation: in the configuration that produced all reported numbers the correction is carried by the anchoring--re-anchoring path, and the relaxation itself is not executed. Extending the state to 6-DoF, narrowing the scale gap with paired real and simulated data, and mapping the attractor dynamics onto neuromorphic hardware are the natural next steps.
