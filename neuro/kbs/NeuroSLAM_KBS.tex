\begin{abstract}
    \sloppy
    Monocular visual-inertial systems usually get the shape of a trajectory right and its scale wrong. A run is consistent only up to a similarity transform, so the scale error propagates into everything downstream, and the accumulated drift can only be removed when the vehicle recognizes ground it has mapped before. We propose NeuroLocMap, a brain-inspired localization-mapping framework extended to 4-DoF state estimation that integrates vestibular--visual complementary fusion with hippocampal--entorhinal spatial representations. It comprises dual-stream visual processing (ventral recognition, dorsal motion), vestibular IMU modeling, a 3D grid-cell continuous attractor for position encoding, and experience-based cognitive mapping for loop closure; on its 10-state EKF core, two corrections complete the system: an online, scale-only monocular correction (a lightweight head over a frozen depth model) and a learned-embedding loop-closure line. On five scenarios --- simulated urban driving (CARLA Town05/03/07/10HD) and real-world highway driving (KITTI~07) --- the full system outperforms the 10-state EKF visual-inertial baseline on all five under both calibers: by 16.5--59.3\% under a shape-only full-Sim(3) refit (an upper bound absorbing global scale) and by 1.5--33.9\% under rigid SE(3), where a wrong metric scale cannot be absorbed. On a dedicated closed-loop benchmark, the experience-map re-anchoring recovers 15$\times$ more accumulated global drift than the EKF baseline (55.92~m vs 842.54~m, anchored Sim(3)). The reference implementation runs at 39--76~FPS on CPU (measured, no GPU); its learned components are trained once on simulation and deployed without per-domain retraining --- the loop embeddings transfer directly, while the scale head's sim-to-real gap is quantified --- and it outputs an interpretable topological experience map.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spatial localization and mapping in complex 3D environments is fundamental to autonomous driving, mobile robotics, and intelligent transportation systems~\cite{cadena2016past}. As the core sensing module of autonomous devices, SLAM directly supports all autonomous movement planning and execution. Modern practical approaches typically combine cameras, IMUs, and sometimes GNSS to achieve robust pose estimation~\cite{saputra2018visual}. Conventional SLAM solutions mostly adopt optimization or filtering frameworks for pose solving, but they commonly have obvious defects in model interpretability and computing resource consumption.

Interestingly, organisms such as bats complete 3D navigation by fusing visual and vestibular signals with hippocampal spatial cells~\cite{jeffery2013navigating,hafting2005microstructure,taube1990head,finkelstein2016three,solstad2008representation}. These neural mechanisms enable brain-inspired localization frameworks to outperform traditional SLAM in interpretability and computational efficiency.

However, translating these biological principles into practical robotic systems faces several challenges. First, most existing bio-inspired frameworks operate in 2D, whereas real-world deployments require robust 3D spatial representation. Second, classical visual template matching is sensitive to lighting and viewpoint variations, limiting place recognition performance in challenging environments. Third, a fusion core that is simultaneously interpretable and cheap --- one whose per-update cost does not grow with the number of visual features --- remains under-explored for brain-inspired localization frameworks.

To address these challenges, we present NeuroLocMap, a brain-inspired localization-mapping framework extended to 4-DoF pose estimation within 3D space (pose $x$, $y$, $z$, $\psi$) that integrates a dual-stream visual front-end (ventral pathway for scene recognition, dorsal pathway for motion extraction) and a vestibular front-end for IMU processing, with a frequency-domain fusion core that instantiates complementary filtering as a fixed-dimension (10-state) EKF. The framework employs 3D grid cells and multilayered head direction cells for spatial representation, and a multilayer experience map functioning as hippocampal-like episodic memory for place recognition and drift correction. For ground vehicles operating on relatively flat terrain (e.g., urban roads, indoor floors), the orientation is dominated by yaw angle $\psi$, while pitch and roll variations are typically constrained by vehicle dynamics ($<5^\circ$). Our 4-DoF representation provides efficient localization-mapping for such scenarios~\cite{milford2004ratslam,milford2010persistent}.

The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The framework couples dual-stream visual processing, vestibular modeling and hippocampal--entorhinal spatial representations into one 4-DoF pipeline rather than stacking them as independent modules.
    \item The visual front-end replaces RatSLAM's handful of templates with an explicit template-creation operator; across the evaluated sequences the count rises from 5 to 964, which is what lets place discrimination survive changes in lighting and viewpoint.
    \item The fusion core costs the same per update no matter how many features are tracked: a fixed-dimension (10-state), loosely coupled EKF, whose gated branches are applied in a fixed order for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:deployed_filter}. The two train-once corrections (a scale-only head over a frozen depth model, and a learned-embedding loop-closure line) sit downstream and never write back into the filter.
    \item On five scenarios (CARLA Town05 / 03 / 07 / 10HD and KITTI~07) the full system beats the 10-state EKF baseline on all five under the rigid SE(3) caliber, where an unrecovered scale cannot be absorbed ($+$1.5--33.9\%), and recovers 15$\times$ more accumulated drift on the closed-loop benchmark (55.92~m vs 842.54~m). The experience map that comes out of this is inspectable, which we treat as a by-product rather than a claim.
\end{itemize}

In knowledge-based terms, the experience map produced by our pipeline serves as an explicit and interpretable topological knowledge representation, and loop closure operates as retrieval over this representation together with consistency-based correction of it.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

All simulated sequences come from CARLA towns collected by ourselves; no third-party SLAM benchmark (e.g., EuRoC) is evaluated, so cross-dataset generalisation of the map and gate parameters remains to be shown.

This study proposes NeuroLocMap, a brain-inspired 4-DoF localization-mapping framework that integrates computational models of 3D grid cells and multilayered head direction cells with dual-stream visual processing and complementary vestibular-visual fusion. Experimental results across five diverse scenarios (CARLA Town 05 / 03 / 07 / 10HD, KITTI~07) verify that NeuroLocMap's full system beats the 10-state EKF visual-inertial baseline on \emph{all five scenarios under both evaluation calibers} and outperforms monocular VO by $+$33.7--68.8\%, while maintaining real-time performance (39--76~FPS, reference implementation). On a dedicated closed-loop benchmark, its experience-map re-anchoring recovers 15$\times$ more of the accumulated global drift than the EKF baseline (55.92~m vs 842.54~m anchored Sim(3)). Future work will extend the framework to full 6-DoF pose representation, close the sim-to-real scale gap, and deploy on neuromorphic hardware to progressively realize interpretable localization systems capable of real-time operation on embedded platforms.
