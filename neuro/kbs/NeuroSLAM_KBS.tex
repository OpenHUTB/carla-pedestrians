\begin{abstract}
    \sloppy
    Brain-inspired spatial cognition offers interpretable alternatives to opaque learning methods for localization, but existing frameworks remain confined to 2D environments or simplified visual processing and lack robust 3D representations. We propose NeuroLocMap, a brain-inspired localization-mapping framework extended to 4-DoF state estimation that integrates vestibular--visual complementary fusion with hippocampal--entorhinal spatial representations. It comprises dual-stream visual processing (ventral recognition, dorsal motion), vestibular IMU modeling, a 3D grid-cell continuous attractor for position encoding, and experience-based cognitive mapping for loop closure; on its 10-state EKF core, two corrections complete the system: an online, scale-only monocular correction (a lightweight head over a frozen depth model) and a learned-embedding loop-closure line. On five scenarios --- simulated urban driving (CARLA Town05/03/07/10HD) and real-world highway driving (KITTI~07) --- the full system outperforms the 10-state EKF visual-inertial baseline on all five under both calibers: by 16.5--59.3\% under a shape-only full-Sim(3) refit (an upper bound absorbing global scale) and by 1.5--33.9\% under rigid SE(3), where a wrong metric scale cannot be absorbed; it also beats monocular visual odometry by 33.7--68.8\%. On a dedicated closed-loop benchmark, the experience-map re-anchoring recovers 15$\times$ more accumulated global drift than the EKF baseline (55.92~m vs 842.54~m, anchored Sim(3)). The reference implementation runs at 39--76~FPS on CPU (measured, no GPU); its learned components are trained once on simulation and deployed without per-domain retraining --- the loop embeddings transfer directly, while the scale head's sim-to-real gap is quantified --- and it outputs an interpretable topological experience map.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Spatial localization and mapping in complex 3D environments is fundamental to autonomous driving, mobile robotics, and intelligent transportation systems~\cite{cadena2016past}. As the core sensing module of autonomous devices, SLAM directly supports all autonomous movement planning and execution. Modern practical approaches typically combine cameras, IMUs, and sometimes GNSS to achieve robust pose estimation~\cite{saputra2018visual}. Conventional SLAM solutions mostly adopt optimization or filtering frameworks for pose solving, but they commonly have obvious defects in model interpretability and computing resource consumption.

Interestingly, organisms such as bats complete 3D navigation by fusing visual and vestibular signals with hippocampal spatial cells~\cite{jeffery2013navigating,hafting2005microstructure,taube1990head,finkelstein2016three,solstad2008representation}. These neural mechanisms enable brain-inspired localization frameworks to outperform traditional SLAM in interpretability and computational efficiency.

However, translating these biological principles into practical robotic systems faces several challenges. First, most existing bio-inspired frameworks operate in 2D, whereas real-world deployments require robust 3D spatial representation. Second, classical visual template matching is sensitive to lighting and viewpoint variations, limiting place recognition performance in challenging environments. Third, a fusion core that is simultaneously interpretable and cheap --- one whose per-update cost does not grow with the number of visual features --- remains under-explored for brain-inspired localization frameworks.

To address these challenges, we present NeuroLocMap, a brain-inspired localization-mapping framework extended to 4-DoF pose estimation within 3D space (pose $x$, $y$, $z$, $\psi$) that integrates a dual-stream visual front-end (ventral pathway for scene recognition, dorsal pathway for motion extraction) and a vestibular front-end for IMU processing, with a frequency-domain fusion core that instantiates complementary filtering as a fixed-dimension (10-state) EKF. The framework employs 3D grid cells and multilayered head direction cells for spatial representation, and a multilayer experience map functioning as hippocampal-like episodic memory for place recognition and drift correction. For ground vehicles operating on relatively flat terrain (e.g., urban roads, indoor floors), the orientation is dominated by yaw angle $\psi$, while pitch and roll variations are typically constrained by vehicle dynamics ($<5^\circ$). Our 4-DoF representation provides efficient localization-mapping for such scenarios~\cite{milford2004ratslam,milford2010persistent}.

The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose a unified brain-inspired architecture that couples dual-stream visual processing with vestibular modeling and hippocampal-entorhinal spatial representations for 4-DoF localization and mapping.
    \item We develop cortex-inspired visual processing with explicit operators for visual template creation, increasing the visual template count from 5 (typical in RatSLAM) to up to 964 (over 192$\times$ improvement) across the evaluation sequences, for finer-grained place discrimination under challenging lighting and viewpoint variations.
    \item We design a lightweight vestibular--visual fusion core whose per-update cost is independent of the number of tracked visual features: the frequency-domain complementarity of the two modalities is instantiated as a fixed-dimension (10-state) loosely coupled EKF whose active gated observation branches are four (a fifth, more conservative heading branch is implemented but disabled in the reported configuration). Two train-once correction branches --- an online scale-only head over a frozen monocular depth model and a learned-embedding loop-closure line --- are attached downstream and deployed zero-shot. The full pipeline runs on CPU only and sustains 39--76~FPS (reference front-end, including evaluation and plotting).
    \item We validate the framework on five diverse scenarios (CARLA Town05 / 03 / 07 / 10HD, KITTI~07): the full system beats the 10-state EKF visual-inertial baseline on \emph{all five scenarios under both evaluation calibers} (Sim(3) shape-only upper bound: $+$16.5--59.3\%; rigid SE(3): $+$1.5--33.9\%), beats monocular VO by $+$33.7--68.8\%, and on a dedicated closed-loop benchmark its experience-map re-anchoring recovers 15$\times$ more of the accumulated global drift than the EKF (55.92~m vs 842.54~m anchored Sim(3)), producing an interpretable topological experience map as a by-product of localization.
\end{itemize}

In knowledge-based terms, the experience map produced by our pipeline serves as an explicit and interpretable topological knowledge representation, and loop closure operates as retrieval over this representation together with consistency-based correction of it.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

All simulated sequences come from CARLA towns collected by ourselves; no third-party SLAM benchmark (e.g., EuRoC) is evaluated, so cross-dataset generalisation of the map and gate parameters remains to be shown.

With the increasing demand for robust and interpretable localization systems in resource-constrained environments, achieving biologically-plausible spatial representations has become essential for autonomous navigation and mobile robotics. This study proposes NeuroLocMap, a brain-inspired 4-DoF localization-mapping framework that integrates computational models of 3D grid cells and multilayered head direction cells with dual-stream visual processing and complementary vestibular-visual fusion. We describe the bio-inspired spatial cell architecture, the dual-stream visual processing strategy inspired by ventral and dorsal pathways, and the lightweight frequency-domain fusion core, implemented as a fixed-dimension EKF, for multi-sensory integration. Experimental results across five diverse scenarios (CARLA Town 05 / 03 / 07 / 10HD, KITTI~07) verify that NeuroLocMap's full system beats the 10-state EKF visual-inertial baseline on \emph{all five scenarios under both evaluation calibers} --- by $+$16.5--59.3\% under the shape-only full-Sim(3) refit and by $+$1.5--33.9\% under the rigid SE(3) alignment, in which a wrong metric scale cannot be absorbed by the aligner --- and outperforms monocular VO by $+$33.7--68.8\%, while maintaining real-time performance (39--76~FPS, reference implementation). On a dedicated closed-loop benchmark, its experience-map re-anchoring recovers 15$\times$ more of the accumulated global drift than the EKF baseline (55.92~m vs 842.54~m anchored Sim(3)). The framework increases the visual template count from $\sim$5~templates (typical in RatSLAM) to up to 964~templates (over 192$\times$ improvement), enabling finer-grained place discrimination for robust loop closure detection. Its learned components --- the monocular scale-correction head and the loop-closure embeddings --- are trained once on simulation and deployed zero-shot on every test sequence, including the real-world one; the loop line transfers directly, while the scale head's current sim-to-real gap is reported honestly. Future work will extend the framework to full 6-DoF pose representation, close the sim-to-real scale gap, and deploy on neuromorphic hardware to progressively realize interpretable localization systems capable of real-time operation on embedded platforms.
