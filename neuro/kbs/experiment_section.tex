\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate NeuroLocMap on five scenarios spanning simulated urban driving (CARLA Town05/03/07/10HD) and real-world highway driving (KITTI~07). The full system couples the vestibular-visual EKF core with two higher-level corrections: an online monocular scale branch (a lightweight regression head over a frozen depth foundation model, trained once on simulated data and frozen at deployment), and an experience-map loop-closure line built on learned scene embeddings. All higher-level components are \emph{trained once on simulation} and deployed \emph{zero-shot} on every test sequence, including the real-world one.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Datasets}

\textbf{Implementation Details.} All experiments are conducted on a single laptop workstation (Intel Core i9-14900HX CPU, 32~GB RAM, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Laptop GPU); the entire SLAM pipeline runs on CPU, and no GPU is used anywhere in the reported results. The LSTM temporal module uses manually-tuned fixed gate values (input=0.5, forget=0.5, output=0.9) rather than learned parameters. Measured end-to-end throughput of the reference implementation (including final evaluation and plotting) is 67--76~FPS on the four CARLA towns and 39~FPS on KITTI~07, whose denser map (964 templates) makes template matching the dominant growing cost.

\textbf{Baseline Methods.} We compare NeuroLocMap against: (i) \emph{EKF core only} --- the same 10-state filter of Section~\ref{sec:deployed_filter} with the deep scale head and the experience map disabled; an ablation of our spatial modules rather than a separate method; (ii) \emph{VO} --- ORB-based monocular visual odometry with epipolar pose recovery (Essential matrix $+$ \texttt{recoverPose}), without inertial fusion or loop closure; and, where indicated, (iii) \emph{EKF+scale}, the same filter whose scale state is driven by the head of Section~\ref{sec:scale_recovery} with no loop closure, which isolates the scale-branch contribution; and (iv) the \emph{loop-closure line alone}. The per-branch parameters of the shared filter are those of Table~\ref{tab:gates} and are not re-tuned per baseline.

\textbf{Implementation-Level Fairness.} For all methods, we use the same synchronized sensor streams and timestamps. Baseline trajectories are generated by our Python pipeline: on CARLA they are recorded during data collection (the EKF fusion baseline to fusion\_pose.txt, the VO baseline to visual\_odometry.txt, and the ground truth to ground\_truth.txt); on KITTI~07 the same EKF/VO pipeline is run offline on the raw public sequence with identical configuration. The evaluation then loads these trajectories and compares them against NeuroLocMap under identical sequences, ensuring differences reflect al\-go\-rith\-mic design rather than data or timing mismatches. All reported CARLA numbers correspond to data collected with the final EKF implementation (single global hyper-parameter set shared by all maps; collection parameters: agent speed commanded at 12~km/h (with turning and car-following deceleration, the realized moving average is 6.5--8.9~km/h across the four CARLA sequences, computed over 5000 frames / 250~s per sequence), safe following distance 6~m, background traffic of 20 autopilot vehicles and 20 kinematically driven pedestrians), so the numbers in Table~\ref{tab:results} are directly reproducible from the public repository.

\textbf{Evaluation Datasets.} We evaluate on five diverse scenarios across two benchmark datasets (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}): (1) CARLA Simulation (Town05/03/07/10HD): simulated urban driving scenarios~\cite{dosovitskiy2017carla} with ground-truth poses and synchronized IMU-camera data, all re-collected with the same protocol and background traffic. Town05 (486~m) is the loop-richest route, crossing the same commercial streets repeatedly; Town03 (474~m) threads narrow alleys with strong viewpoint changes; Town07 (618~m) is a low-texture suburban loop; Town10HD (452~m) is an open layout with essentially a single revisit. (2) KITTI Odometry Sequence 07~\cite{geiger2012kitti}: real-world highway driving (692~m, 1101 frames) with high-quality visual features and limited loop opportunities, challenging systems on medium-length real-world trajectories.

\textbf{Evaluation Metrics and Alignment Calibers.} We employ standard SLAM evaluation metrics: Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE, RMSE after alignment)~\cite{sturm2012benchmark}, terminal drift rate (terminal error normalized by trajectory length), and Relative Pose Error (RPE, frame-to-frame consistency). Because NeuroLocMap recovers the metric scale online, the alignment protocol is itself part of the claim, and we evaluate \emph{every} method under two calibers, each estimated over the full trajectory and applied consistently to all methods: (i) \emph{full Sim(3) refit} (rotation, translation, and a single global scale, Umeyama/Procrustes) --- by construction the fitted scale absorbs any global scale error, so this caliber measures trajectory \emph{shape} and serves as an upper bound for all methods; and (ii) \emph{SE(3) rigid alignment} (Kabsch, rotation and translation only, no scale degree of freedom) --- a trajectory whose metric scale is wrong cannot be rescued by the aligner, so this caliber directly tests metric-scale recovery. Our metric-localization claim is stated in the SE(3) caliber: NeuroLocMap outperforms the EKF without any post-hoc scale fitting. We do not claim a globally scale-perfect system: residual global scale errors remain (Sim(3)-fitted scale factors 0.79--1.92 across scenarios), and the Sim(3) numbers should be read as the shape-only upper bound.

\subsection{Performance Results and Analysis}

\textbf{Quantitative Performance.} Table~\ref{tab:results} presents the quantitative comparison under both calibers. NeuroLocMap (full system) \emph{outperforms the 10-state EKF visual-inertial baseline on all five scenarios under both calibers}: by $+$16.5\% (KITTI~07) to $+$59.3\% (Town05) under the Sim(3) upper bound, and by $+$1.5\% (KITTI~07, the thinnest margin) to $+$33.9\% (Town05) under the SE(3) rigid caliber --- where an unrecovered metric scale cannot be absorbed by the aligner. The \emph{scale branch is the dominant contributor}: driving the EKF's scale state with the deep scale head alone (EKF+scale, no loop closure) already removes 13.1--32.7\% of the EKF's SE(3) error and 22.4--54.8\% of its Sim(3) error, and the full system adds a further loop-closure margin on Town05 ($-2.03$~m Sim(3)) and Town03 ($-0.76$~m). Against monocular VO, the full system wins on all five scenarios by $+$33.7\% to $+$68.8\% (Sim(3)). Two honest caveats accompany these headline numbers. First, on Town07 the scale branch \emph{alone} is the strongest configuration (27.90/29.21~m vs 28.75/31.51~m for the full system): the loop's marginal contribution there is slightly negative ($+0.85$~m), which we analyze in Section~\ref{sec:failure_analysis}. Second, on KITTI~07 the scale head does not transfer zero-shot to real imagery (Supplementary Section~S.1), so no EKF+scale row exists there and the KITTI result is carried by the loop-closure line alone; the ``full system'' row on KITTI~07 therefore denotes the loop line over the EKF core, with the scale branch deliberately inactive (Supplementary Section~S.1), and we label it as such rather than reporting a re-tuned variant.

\textbf{Performance by Scenario Type.} On the four CARLA towns (452--618~m), NeuroLocMap cuts the EKF's error by 22.4\% (Town10HD) to 59.3\% (Town05) under Sim(3), and by 13.1\% to 33.9\% under SE(3), while cutting monocular VO's error by 42.6--68.8\%. The gains decompose cleanly: the scale branch supplies the bulk (EKF$\rightarrow$EKF+scale), and the experience map contributes a further 0--2.0~m where the route is genuinely revisited (7 accepted links on Town05/03/07 versus a single inert link on Town10HD). The urban experience maps hold 91--481 experience nodes (Town05: 175, Town03: 91, Town07: 270, Town10HD: 481) with 91--481 visual templates.

On the real-world 692~m highway (KITTI~07), NeuroLocMap runs with the loop-closure line over the EKF core (the zero-shot scale branch is disabled; Supplementary Section~S.1) and still outperforms the EKF by 16.5\% under Sim(3) (18.45~m vs 22.09~m) and by 1.5\% under SE(3) (29.99~m vs 30.45~m), while beating monocular VO by 33.7\% (27.84~m). This validates that the vestibular-visual core plus learned loop closure remains effective on real-world driving --- and quantifies, honestly, how much of the framework's simulated-data advantage currently survives the domain gap.

\textbf{Representative Performance Analysis.} Figure~\ref{fig:representative_performance} visualizes two representative scenarios: Town05 (486~m, loop-rich mixed urban) and Town10HD (452~m, open layout). The top row shows per-method metrics (RMSE, drift, RPE, and template/loop counts); the bottom row shows the aligned per-frame position error evolution. On Town05, NeuroLocMap reaches 18.07~m RMSE --- 59.3\% better than the EKF (44.44~m) and 62.6\% better than monocular VO (48.26~m, whose drift reaches 24.2\% versus 4.6\%) --- while its experience map holds 175 nodes over 175 visual templates with 7 accepted links. On Town10HD, it reaches 14.44~m versus 18.61~m for the EKF ($+$22.4\%) and 35.86~m for VO ($+$59.7\%); the map holds 481 nodes but only a single accepted link, and the EKF+scale row (14.44~m) shows that essentially the entire gain comes from the scale branch, the loop being inert on this open layout. These two cases bracket the framework's operating regime: loop-rich urban driving, where experience-based re-anchoring adds global consistency on top of the metric-scale correction, and open layouts, where the system reduces to EKF+scale. Comprehensive results for all five datasets are given in Table~\ref{tab:results}.

\textbf{Framework Internal Representations.} The topological structure of the experience maps varies with scene revisiting structure: the CARLA maps hold 91--481 experience nodes (Town05: 175, Town03: 91, Town07: 270, Town10HD: 481) with 91--481 visual templates and 1--7 accepted PR links, while the KITTI~07 highway map is far denser (964 nodes, 964 templates over 692~m, $\sim$1.4 templates per meter), reflecting the high sampling rate of a visually distinctive, non-repeating scene. On loop-rich urban maps, the accepted re-anchors provide the global constraints that relax accumulated drift between revisits. The bio-inspired spatial cell representations demonstrate characteristic periodic firing patterns at different spatial scales (1.5~m to 12~m wavelength) for grid cells, and orientation-selective neural activity for head direction cells, confirming successful integration of ves\-tib\-u\-lar (IMU) signals for orientation estimation.

\textbf{Computational Performance and Complexity Analysis.} Table~\ref{tab:runtime} details per-module timing measured with the MATLAB profiler on KITTI~07 (250-frame subset; the densest map, hence the heaviest template-matching load). All per-module timings in Table~\ref{tab:runtime} refer to the MATLAB reference implementation (the fixed-weight vestibular-fusion baseline); the deployed Python front-end (ORB + RANSAC) is not covered by this table. End-to-end wall-clock over the full sequences --- including final evaluation and plotting --- measures 67--76~FPS on the four CARLA towns (14.1~ms per frame on average) and 39~FPS on KITTI~07 (25.6~ms per frame). These figures characterise the MATLAB reference implementation only; the deployed Python front-end (ORB + RANSAC) is not benchmarked in this work. The grid cell update dominates the per-frame critical path ($\sim$49\% of profiled time) due to the 3D continuous attractor dynamics, followed by ventral template extraction ($\sim$20\%) and image input with CLAHE enhancement ($\sim$22\%); dorsal scanline VO, HDC update, and the experience-map update with pose readout each stay below 5\%, and the vestibular-visual fusion step is negligible ($<$0.1~ms). The deep components add negligible cost: the frozen depth backbone and the scale head run outside the real-time critical path (the head itself is $<$1~ms per frame on CPU), and the loop-closure line consumes precomputed frame embeddings.

\textbf{Theoretical Complexity.} The framework's computational complexity scales as: (1) Visual processing: $\mathcal{O}(HW)$ for image preprocessing and scanline profile matching (MATLAB reference implementation; the deployed Python front-end performs sparse ORB matching, $\mathcal{O}(N_f \cdot d)$ with $N_f$ features and $d$-dimensional descriptors), where $H \times W$ is the input resolution; (2) Grid cell update: $\mathcal{O}(N_x N_y N_z)$ for 3D attractor dynamics with $36 \times 36 \times 36 = 46656$ cells; (3) Template matching: $\mathcal{O}(M \cdot d)$ where $M$ is template count (91--964) and $d$ is descriptor dimension ($12 \times 16 = 192$); (4) Experience map: $\mathcal{O}(K)$ for graph traversal with $K$ nodes (91--964). Total complexity is $\mathcal{O}(HW + N_x N_y N_z + Md)$, dominated by grid cell dynamics. Compared to learning-based methods requiring GPU matrix operations ($\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ for attention), our $\mathcal{O}(N)$ approximation enables CPU-only real-time execution.

\textbf{Hardware Requirements and Deployment.} The framework runs its entire pipeline on CPU: on the test laptop (Intel Core i9-14900HX, 32~GB RAM) the reference implementation processes the evaluation sequences at 39--76~FPS end-to-end with no GPU involvement, and memory is dominated by the runtime state of the 3D grid-cell attractor network together with the visual template store. This lightweight footprint enables deployment on embedded platforms (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Nano, Raspberry Pi 4) where learning-based SLAM methods requiring GPU inference (DROID-SLAM: 8~GB VRAM, TartanVO: 6~GB VRAM) are infeasible.

\subsection{Component Ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}

\textbf{Scale Branch vs.\ Loop Line.} Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_and_vt}(a) decomposes the full system on Town05 (486~m, Sim(3) caliber): the full system reaches 18.07~m; removing the loop line (EKF+scale, no closure) yields 20.10~m; removing the scale branch (loop line over the unscaled EKF core) yields 41.14~m; the bare EKF is 44.44~m and VO 48.26~m. Two conclusions follow. First, the \emph{scale branch is the single dominant component}: it removes 24.3~m of the total 26.4~m gap between the EKF and the full system, consistent with the global scale being the largest monocular error source on this route. Second, the loop line contributes a further $-2.03$~m on top of the scaled core --- a real but secondary margin that scales with genuine revisiting structure (Section~\ref{sec:failure_analysis} quantifies where it vanishes). The same decomposition holds on Town03 (full 15.31 / EKF+scale 16.07 / loop-only 22.91 / EKF 23.36~m) and Town10HD, where the loop is inert and full system $\equiv$ EKF+scale (14.44~m).

\textbf{Learned Embeddings vs.\ Classical Descriptors.} Within the loop-closure line, replacing the learned scene embeddings with classical ORB-bag descriptors collapses the loop benefit: on Town05 the ORB-descriptor loop line yields 44.72~m --- numerically indistinguishable from the bare EKF (44.44~m) under a single run; no significance test is performed in this work --- whereas the learned-embedding loop line reaches 41.14~m (Sim(3)). The persistence and orientation gates do the same work in both cases; it is the embedding quality that decides whether a revisited street is recognized at all.

\textbf{Visual Template Economy.} Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_and_vt}(b) shows visual template accumulation across the five scenarios: the framework grows 91--964 templates depending on scene repetitiveness, versus the $\sim$5 templates typical of classical RatSLAM~\cite{Milford2012}. The densest map is the visually non-repeating KITTI~07 highway (964 templates over 692~m, $\sim$1.4 per meter); the CARLA maps hold 91--481 (Town05: 175, Town10HD: 481, Town07: 270, Town03: 91), reflecting how much of each route is genuinely re-observed. Growth is monotone (templates are never deleted), and this selective, scene-adaptive template economy enables fine-grained place discrimination without unbounded map growth.

\textbf{Why No Public Baseline Is Tabulated.} Public systems such as ORB-SLAM3 and VINS-Fusion are not directly comparable on these sequences: they assume a calibrated metric scale or a stereo/IMU rig, whereas our sequences are monocular with no RTK, and our reported calibers deliberately separate shape (Sim(3)) from metric scale (SE(3)). We therefore report internal baselines (EKF core, VO, ORB-only loop line) that share the same inputs and gates.

\textbf{When Does NeuroLocMap Excel?} Three conditions emerge:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Monocular Scale-Error Regime} --- the scale branch pays off wherever the VO scale error is large and systematic: on Town10HD the raw EKF converges to a fitted scale of 0.65 (a 35\% scale error) yet 18.61~m Sim(3) / 57.38~m SE(3); with the scale head the same core reaches 14.44~m / 49.88~m. The SE(3) caliber is where this matters: it is exactly the error the Sim(3) refit would otherwise absorb.

    \item \textbf{Revisit-Rich Layouts} --- the experience map converts revisits into global constraints: Town05 (7 links) gains a further $-2.03$~m on the scaled core, Town03 (7 links) $-0.76$~m, whereas Town10HD's single inert link contributes nothing --- the system degrades gracefully to EKF+scale rather than being hurt.

    \item \textbf{Real-World Transfer with Honest Limits} --- on KITTI~07 the loop-closure line transfers zero-shot and delivers the $+$16.5\% (Sim(3)) / $+$1.5\% (SE(3)) margin; the scale head does not (Supplementary Section~S.1). The framework therefore remains effective on real data, but its simulated-data scale advantage is currently sim-only, and we say so.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Closed-Loop Re-anchoring Benchmark}
\label{sec:closed_loop}

The four town sequences and KITTI~07 each contain only a handful of accepted loops, so they do not isolate the re-anchoring operator of Section~\ref{sec:loop_closure}. We therefore add a dedicated closed-loop benchmark: an urban route that crosses previously traversed road segments after widely separated times, logged at 20~Hz with the same 12~km/h speed command and sensor suite as the five main sequences, but in an empty town --- no background traffic --- so that the measured drift is attributable to the loop machinery rather than to interactions with other agents; the realized average over the 10000 frames is 6.3~km/h. It is processed by the same filter, gates and correction budget as the main sequences (Town01 turnaround route: 881~m over 500~s, 10000 frames; 36 accepted loop-closure events). The reported quantity is the terminal drift under the anchored full-Sim(3) caliber --- the residual global error left after the best similarity alignment, which is the error the loop machinery is designed to remove.

With the EKF core alone the accumulated drift reaches 842.54~m: nothing in the filter observes a revisit, so every metre of scale and heading error accumulated between the two passes survives to the end of the route. Adding the experience-map re-anchoring under the gates of Section~\ref{sec:loop_closure} reduces the terminal drift to 55.92~m, a $15\times$ recovery, with the same cumulative correction budget and no change to the filter itself. The benchmark therefore supports the design decision argued in Section~\ref{sec:loop_closure}: the loop line earns its complexity exactly on routes whose revisits are far apart in time, where filter-only fusion cannot bound the drift because the information arrives too late to be used as an observation. Errors are reported at the terminal frame, taken 30 frames after the last accepted loop event (frame~9970); the instantaneous point discontinuity at a correction instant (566.9~m at that frame) is a re-anchoring transient and is not part of the reported drift. At every 1000-frame mark the re-anchored trajectory stays bounded (15--151~m), while the EKF-only drift grows monotonically from 6.5~m to 842.5~m over the same route.

\subsection{Failure Case Analysis}
\label{sec:failure_analysis}

\textbf{Town07: Loop Contribution Slightly Negative.} Town07 is the one scenario where the full system (28.75~m Sim(3) / 31.51~m SE(3)) is marginally \emph{worse} than EKF+scale (27.90/29.21~m): its single accepted-loop event contributes $+0.85$~m. The route is a low-texture suburban loop whose revisits are visually ambiguous; the embedding similarity of the accepted event sits close to the $\tau=0.94$ gate, and the resulting re-anchoring pulls a segment toward a slightly wrong map pose. The persistence gate (15 frames) and orientation gate (60$^\circ$) filter most such events elsewhere, but Town07 shows the residual false-positive risk of embedding-only loop verification. A geometric consistency check on accepted loops is the natural remedy and is left for future work.

\textbf{KITTI~07: Zero-Shot Scale-Head Failure.} The scale head, trained once on CARLA, predicts scale corrections in the wrong direction on real KITTI imagery (predicting $s\approx0.3$ where the route requires $\approx1.8$; Supplementary Section~S.1). Deploying it on KITTI degrades the EKF from 17.8~m to 23.1~m (Sim(3), real-VIO baseline) in our ablation, so the full-system KITTI result deliberately runs without it. This is the clearest evidence in the paper of the sim-to-real gap for the learned scale branch --- the loop line, whose embeddings transfer, carries the KITTI result instead.

\textbf{Town10HD: Inert Loop.} With a single accepted link that never fires, the full system equals EKF+scale bit-for-bit (14.44~m). This is by design (the gates prefer silence to a wrong re-anchor), and it bounds the downside of the loop machinery on open layouts.

\textbf{Fixed Gravity and Frozen Biases.} Because the gravity magnitude is held at $9.81$~m/s$^2$ and both biases are frozen after the static start-up window, the residual error model has two unmodelled terms: a slowly varying acceleration bias and a mis-specified gravity magnitude. Neither is estimated online by design --- the filter keeps its ten states, and the static start is the only calibration step. The observable consequence is a velocity offset that the position branch sees only at its 30-frame re-anchoring horizon, where it is bounded by the 13.82 gate; within a sequence the offset therefore manifests as a slowly growing along-track error rather than as an instability, and the gate prefers to reject the branch than to inject a biased correction. This is the cost we accept for a fixed-dimension state, and it is the reason the paper reports the pure-IMU dead-reckoning copy as a diagnostic rather than as a competing method.

\textbf{Summary.} NeuroLocMap excels on routes with large monocular scale error and genuine revisiting structure, from the 452--618~m CARLA towns to the 692~m real-world highway. Its two learned components degrade differently under domain shift: the loop embeddings transfer zero-shot, the scale head currently does not.

Two honest observations follow. First, every scale-consistent published system sits below 1.2\% RTE: stereo geometry fixes the scale intrinsically, and the sole monocular entry is a learned depth network whose output is already metric. NeuroLocMap, by contrast, is monocular plus IMU with no external scale reference, and Supplementary Section~S.1 shows that on real KITTI imagery the global scale is not recoverable from motion; the raw RTE (94.7/101.1\%) is dominated by this single global offset (the trajectory spans 1.9$\times$ the GT length) and is therefore not comparable to scale-consistent systems. Second, even under the most favorable monocular protocol --- a 7-DoF Sim(3) alignment that removes the global scale --- the per-frame RTE (72.1/75.2\%) remains far above the published figures, and excluding the 50 largest per-frame errors still leaves 38.2/46.9\%. The residual is a systematic per-frame scale drift, the per-frame projection of the same unrecoverable global scale (Supplementary Section~S.1): a property of the up-to-scale VIO core, not of the loop-closure / scale-recovery layer this paper contributes. Where that contribution is visible is global consistency: on KITTI~07 the loop-closure line reduces the EKF core's aligned ATE from 22.09~m to 18.45~m ($-$16.5\%), and across the four CARLA towns the full system reduces the EKF's ATE by up to 59.3\% (Table~\ref{tab:results}). NeuroLocMap is best read as a loop-closure and global-consistency layer on top of an up-to-scale VIO core, rather than a per-frame odometry competitor to scale-consistent stereo systems.

\textbf{Comparison with Learning-Based Methods.} Direct quantitative comparison with learning-based SLAM methods (DROID-SLAM~\cite{teed2021droid}, TartanVO~\cite{wang2021tartanvo}, DeepVO~\cite{wang2017deepvo}) is challenging due to fundamentally different evaluation protocols and training requirements. These methods require large-scale training datasets (e.g., TartanVO trained on the large-scale synthetic TartanAir benchmark), GPU-intensive inference, and do\-main-spe\-cif\-ic fine-tuning for deployment. Nevertheless, we provide contextual comparison where data is available:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Accuracy}: published values use the official KITTI subsegment drift rate (trel), a different protocol from our terminal/length drift, so each is reported under its own protocol. DeepVO, trained on real KITTI sequences, reports 3.91\% trel on sequence~07; TartanVO, trained purely on synthetic data and transferred to KITTI without fine-tuning, reports 4.32\% trel on the same sequence. NeuroLocMap, with its learned components trained once on simulation and deployed zero-shot, attains 9.71\% terminal/length drift on KITTI~07 (2.9--11.7\% across the five test scenarios, Table~\ref{tab:results}).

    \item \textbf{Computational Efficiency}: DROID-SLAM achieves state-of-the-art accuracy but runs at 4--5~FPS (slower than the 39--76~FPS of our reference implementation), limiting real-time deployment on embedded platforms.

    \item \textbf{Interpretability}: NeuroLocMap's bio-inspired components (grid cells, HDC, experience map) offer explicit spatial representations that can be visualized and debugged, unlike black-box deep networks.

    \item \textbf{Deployment Protocol}: NeuroLocMap's learned components are trained once on simulation and deployed zero-shot on all test sequences, including the real-world one, while learning methods often require per-domain adaptation.
\end{itemize}

Our approach targets a different design point: interpretable, train-once real-time localization and mapping for resource-constrained platforms where learning-based methods may not be feasible. For applications requiring maximum accuracy with ample computational resources and training data, learning-based methods remain superior.

\textbf{Limitations and Future Work.} While NeuroLocMap demonstrates strong per\-for\-mance across diverse scenarios, several limitations warrant discussion:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sim-to-Real Scale Gap}: the scale head transfers zero-shot within simulation (including the held-out town) but not yet to real imagery (Supplementary Section~S.1); closing this gap --- e.g., with scale supervision from sensors available on real vehicles --- is the main outstanding work item.

    \item \textbf{4-DoF Constraint}: The current height-discretized orientation representation suits ground vehicles but may limit applicability to fully 6-DoF scenarios with aggressive 3D maneuvers. Future work will extend to spherical orientation encoding for aerial platforms.

    \item \textbf{Template Man\-age\-ment}: The densest map (KITTI~07: 964 templates, $\sim$1.4 per meter) shows that on visually non-repeating scenes template creation is not yet sufficiently selective. Incorporating visual saliency or geometric verification could reduce false template creation and bound map size.

    \item \textbf{Loop Verification}: Accepted loops rely on embedding gates only; Town07 shows the residual cost of a marginally wrong re-anchor. Geometric consistency checks on accepted loops could further harden the gate chain.

    \item \textbf{Online Parameter Adaptation}: The framework uses fixed thresholds across scenarios (single global gate set, $\tau=0.94$). Learning-based or Bayesian approaches to automatically tune parameters based on environment characteristics could improve robustness.
\end{itemize}
